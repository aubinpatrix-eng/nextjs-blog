We often prepare each ATHX zone separately: strength on one side, cardio on the other. But in competition, the hardest part is often **moving from one zone to the next**, especially from strength to endurance.

## Why it's so hard

After heavy lifting, your heart rate is high, your legs are loaded and the adrenaline drops. The first kilometre of running then feels endless. That's when many athletes go out too fast out of nerves, or on the contrary lose heart.

## Three mental strategies

1. **Break the effort down**: don't think "1h30 to go", think "I finish this kilometre". Short goals make the effort manageable.
2. **Plan a starting pace**: decide in advance on a deliberately cautious pace for the first 5 minutes. You'll speed up afterwards if you feel good.
3. **Prepare a key phrase**: a word or short phrase ("relaxed", "steady") that you repeat when your head starts to give in. It sounds simple, but it's very effective.

## Training the transitions

Your mindset is also built in training. Once a week or every two weeks, go from a strength session into a running or rowing block **without rest**. You'll get to know these sensations, and they'll scare you much less on race day. That's the lesson I learned from my first ATHX: read [my Paris race report](/blog/athx-paris-2026-race-report).

For a first example of this kind of combination, follow the [free ATHX program](/free-athx-training-week): its Metcon session puts you in race conditions from week one.

## On competition day

- Use the Refuel zone to breathe, drink and **visualise** the next zone ([what to eat in the Refuel zone](/blog/what-to-eat-refuel-zone)).
- Don't watch the other athletes at the start of the Endurance zone: stick to your plan.
- Save energy for [Metcon X](/blog/metcon-x-explained), that's where places are won.
- Check out the venue the day before if you can: our [ATHX Montpellier](/athx-montpellier-training) guide explains how to organise your arrival at an event far from home.
