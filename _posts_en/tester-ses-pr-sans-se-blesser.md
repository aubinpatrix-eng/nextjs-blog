To train using percentages of your max, you need to know that max. But testing a 1RM (one-rep max) carries risks, especially if you're not used to it. Here's how to do it smartly.

## Option 1: estimate your 1RM

This is the safest method. Build up gradually to a load you can lift **3 to 5 times cleanly**, then use the Epley formula:

> 1RM ≈ load × (1 + reps ÷ 30)

Example: 90 kg × 4 reps → 90 × (1 + 4/30) ≈ **102 kg**.

The estimate is reliable as long as you stay under 6 reps.

## Option 2: test a true 1RM

If you have experience with heavy lifting:

1. Warm up for 10 to 15 minutes (light cardio + mobility).
2. Do ramp-up sets: 8 reps at 40%, 5 at 60%, 3 at 75%, 1 at 85%, 1 at 92%.
3. Go for your max, then 1 or 2 more attempts adding 2.5 to 5 kg.
4. Stop as soon as your technique breaks down.

Take 3 to 5 minutes of rest between heavy attempts, and use a spotter (or safety bars).

For the technique of each lift, see our [back squat](/athx-movements/back-squat), [deadlift](/athx-movements/deadlift) and [shoulder-to-overhead](/athx-movements/shoulder-to-overhead) pages.

## When to test?

Ideally **10 to 12 weeks before the competition**, at the start of your preparation. Never test a new max in the 10 days before the race.

In practice: around 23 November 2026 for [ATHX Paris training](/athx-paris-training), 8 March 2027 for [ATHX Montpellier](/athx-montpellier-training) and 21 June 2027 for the [ATHX Marseille program](/athx-marseille-training).
