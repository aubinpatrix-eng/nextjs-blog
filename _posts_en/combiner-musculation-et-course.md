"Running is going to cost me kilos on the bar." That's the number one fear of lifters getting into ATHX, Hyrox or hybrid fitness. It's not completely wrong, but it's greatly exaggerated. With a few simple rules, you can improve at both at the same time.

## The interference effect: what you really need to know

When you mix strength and endurance training, it's called **concurrent training**. Studies show it can slow some gains, especially **power** and, to a lesser extent, muscle and strength gains. But this interference mainly depends on three factors:

1. **Endurance volume**: a few sessions a week hardly get in the way. It's large volumes, in kilometres and frequency, that cause problems.
2. **Type of effort**: running, with its impact, tires the legs more than cycling, rowing or the SkiErg.
3. **Recovery**: sleep, protein and carb intake, and how sessions are spread over the week.

In other words, the problem isn't running, it's **getting the dose wrong**. And for ATHX, you don't need marathon volume: the Endurance zone run is 3 km in ATHX and Pro.

## The 5 rules to improve at both

### 1. Separate the sessions when you can

Ideally, strength and running **at least 6 hours apart**, for example lifting at lunchtime and running in the evening, or on different days. Your heavy sets are done on fresh legs.

### 2. If it's the same session, strength first

When you only have one slot, start with your priority. For a strength athlete preparing for ATHX, that's strength: heavy sets need freshness, running tolerates fatigue better.

### 3. Keep most of your running easy

About **80% of your running volume at an easy pace**, where you can talk, and the rest as hard sessions (threshold, intervals). It's accumulated hard sessions that tire the legs, not easy running.

### 4. Use the rower and the SkiErg

They build your cardio engine with far less impact than running. They also feature directly at ATHX: the [SkiErg](/athx-movements/skierg) in the Endurance zone and the [rower](/athx-movements/rower) in Metcon X.

### 5. Eat and sleep for two goals

Two types of training mean more energy spent. A prolonged calorie deficit is the surest way to lose strength. Aim for protein at every meal, carbs around your sessions, and 7 to 9 hours of sleep.

## A sample week

| Day | Session |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Strength: back squat + overhead press |
| Tuesday | Easy run, 30 to 45 min |
| Wednesday | Strength: deadlift + bench press |
| Thursday | Intervals: 5 × 1 km at threshold pace, or SkiErg |
| Friday | Light strength + rower |
| Saturday | Combination: heavy squats then a 3 km run, or a Metcon simulation |
| Sunday | Rest |

Strength loads are given as a **percentage of your PRs**: calculate them with the [1RM calculator](/1rm-calculator). For a full, detailed week, with sets, rest and coaching cues, [try the free ATHX program](/free-athx-training-week).

## The special case of ATHX: learning to link efforts

Most of the time, you separate strength and running to improve at both. But ATHX asks you to **run right after lifting heavy**. So once a week or every two weeks, you also need to do exactly the opposite: deliberately link the two, without rest. That's training under **pre-fatigue**.

I learned this the hard way in Paris: without this specific work, I lost on endurance what I'd gained on strength. I tell the story in [my ATHX Paris 2026 race report](/blog/athx-paris-2026-race-report), and the mental side is covered in [managing the strength → endurance transition](/blog/strength-to-endurance-transition).

## In summary

- Two to three runs a week are compatible with getting stronger.
- Separate the sessions, or do strength first.
- Keep most of your running easy, and add the rower and SkiErg.
- Keep one combination session to prepare for the competition format.
