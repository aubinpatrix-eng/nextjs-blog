Metcon X is the last ATHX block. It comes after strength, endurance work and the recovery zones, in other words when your tank is already well drained. That's exactly why it weighs so much in the ranking: it doesn't just measure your ability to produce an intense effort, but your ability to produce it **tired**.

## What's in it

"Metcon" is short for *metabolic conditioning*: a high-intensity effort linking several functional movements with no real break. At ATHX, it includes bodyweight movements, sandbag work and carries. The exact content can change from one edition to the next: always check the official rules of your event before planning your preparation. For the 2027 season, the station-by-station detail is in [the 2027 Metcon X](/athx-2027-workouts/metcon-x).

## Why it all comes down to this

In the first zones, the gaps between athletes of the same level often stay small. In Metcon X, they widen for three reasons:

1. **Accumulated fatigue**: those who pushed too hard in the Strength or Endurance zone pay the price.
2. **Breaking technique**: a poorly mastered movement becomes very costly when your cardio is in the red.
3. **Pacing**: going out too fast on a short, intense format often leads to blowing up halfway.

## How to prepare for it

The best preparation for Metcon X isn't doing metcons every day. It's **placing short, intense sessions after some prior work**, to reproduce the real fatigue of the competition. For example:

- 20 minutes of moderate strength work (squat, overhead press);
- 15 to 20 minutes of running or rowing at a solid pace;
- then a short 8 to 12-minute high-intensity circuit.

One session like this every one to two weeks is enough. The rest of the time, you build the strength and engine that make these simulations possible. For the mental side of these combinations, also read [managing the strength → endurance transition](/blog/strength-to-endurance-transition).
