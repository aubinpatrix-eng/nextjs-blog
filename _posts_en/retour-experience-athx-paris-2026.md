In April 2026, I took part in my first ATHX, in Paris, at the Paris Event Center. It was also the competition's first Paris edition. I have great memories of it, and a strength result I'm proud of: **490 kg in total**, the best at the Paris event. But it's mostly what didn't go to plan that taught me the most. Here's my unfiltered race report, so you can avoid the same traps.

## The 2026 format

To understand what follows: in 2026, the [Strength zone](/athx-2027-workouts/strength-zone) required a 1RM strict overhead press, a 3RM back squat and a 5RM deadlift. The Endurance zone came next, after a short Refuel zone, with alternating running and rowing. In between, barely time to catch your breath.

I totalled **70 kg on the strict press, 200 kg on the squat and 220 kg on the deadlift**. To see how this total compares with the other participants, check our [ATHX 2026 results](/athx-results) page.

## The Warm-up zone: no hooks on the racks

ATHX gives you 30 minutes of warm-up before the Strength zone. On paper, that's plenty of time to build up gradually to your first attempts.

On the day, the warm-up area wasn't ready: **the racks had no hooks**. No way to set a bar at shoulder height. So I couldn't do a single set of squats or overhead presses before starting the competition. I warmed up lightly, with bodyweight work and a bit of mobility, and that was it.

I'm not blaming anyone: it was the very beginning of the brand in France, and a first edition is never perfectly polished. But it's part of what a competition can throw at you, and you need to be ready for it.

## The consequence: I ramped up during the event

Once in the Strength zone, I had no choice: **I had to do my whole ramp-up during the event**. Instead of going straight to attempts close to my max, I had to go through intermediate sets, on the squat and the press, to bring my body gradually up to heavy loads.

The total followed: 490 kg. But all those extra reps come at a cost. I left the Strength zone with my legs already well drained.

## The Endurance zone: I blew up

That's where the bill arrived. From the first minutes of running, my legs no longer responded like they did in training. I never found my pace, and I lost on endurance many of the places I'd gained on strength.

Looking back, two things explain the gap:

1. **The fatigue from the ramp-up**, done in competition instead of in the warm-up.
2. **I hadn't trained the combination enough**: running right after lifting heavy, on already loaded legs.

The first point was out of my hands. The second wasn't.

## What I'd do differently

### 1. Arrive with a back-up warm-up

Today, I'd arrive with **a written warm-up plan that doesn't depend on any rack**:

- 5 to 8 minutes of hip, ankle and shoulder mobility;
- **goblet squats** with a dumbbell or kettlebell, getting heavier;
- **light presses and push presses** with a bar taken from the floor, to wake up the shoulders;
- a few explosive reps (jumps, medicine ball throws) to fire up the nervous system.

And I'd check out the warm-up area as soon as I arrived, to know straight away whether my plan A is possible. The full minute-by-minute protocol is in [warming up before a 1RM](/blog/warm-up-before-1rm).

### 2. Keep the ramp-up to a strict minimum

If the warm-up goes wrong anyway, the ramp-up during the event must be **short**: few sets, big load jumps, 1 to 2 reps per set, then a first attempt at around 90% of your max. Every extra set is paid for in the next zone. To plan your steps in advance, use the [1RM calculator](/1rm-calculator).

### 3. Train under pre-fatigue

This is the real lesson of my day. At ATHX, you never run on fresh legs. So you need to train to **lift heavy, then go straight out running**, without rest. That's what we call training under pre-fatigue.

In practice, once a week or every two weeks:

- 4 to 5 heavy sets of squats or deadlifts (3 to 5 reps at 80-85%);
- then, within a minute, **a 3 km run** at your race pace;
- and to match the 2027 format, a few minutes of SkiErg afterwards.

The first sessions are hard, and that's normal: it's exactly what race day feels like. Over the weeks, your legs and your head get used to it. The mental side of this combination is covered in [managing the strength → endurance transition](/blog/strength-to-endurance-transition).

That's precisely why I built the [ATHX PREP program](/#cta) around these **pre-fatigue sessions**: heavy loads calculated from your PRs, followed straight away by running, rowing or SkiErg (the program is written in French). To see what a week looks like, [try the free ATHX program](/free-athx-training-week).

## And for 2027?

The format changes in 2027: the Strength zone becomes a **1RM shoulder-to-overhead, 2RM squat and 3RM deadlift**, and the Endurance zone becomes **a 3 km run then as many SkiErg metres as possible** within 24 minutes. My two lessons matter even more:

- with fewer reps and heavier loads, **the warm-up becomes decisive**: you don't go up to your 1RM without a proper ramp-up;
- the run still comes **right after strength**, and every second lost over the 3 km is time lost on the SkiErg. Estimate your distance with the [Endurance zone calculator](/athx-endurance-zone-calculator).
