Sandbag work is very common in functional formats like Metcon X. On paper it looks simple. In reality, an unstable sandbag with no solid handle surprises many beginners and tires them out far more than expected.

## Why it's hard

Unlike a barbell or a kettlebell, a sandbag **moves**: the sand shifts, the grip is imperfect, and every rep works your forearms, core and back differently.

## The basic technique

To pick up the bag and get it to your shoulder:

1. Feet hip-width apart, bag between your feet.
2. Hips back, back flat, grab the bag from underneath by hugging it.
3. Bring the bag up onto your thighs, then drive hard with your hips to launch it to your shoulder.
4. Alternate shoulders from one rep to the next.

The secret: your **hips** do the work, not your arms.

## Common mistakes

- **Lifting with a rounded back** and your arms: the fast track to back pain.
- **Gripping too hard**: you burn out your forearms for what comes next.
- **Never training with a sandbag**: if your gym doesn't have one, a sports bag filled with bags of sand or rice will do.

In 2027, the sandbag is used for two Metcon X stations: 30 to 45 squats then a 60 m carry, with a 20 to 70 kg bag depending on the category (see [the 2027 Metcon X](/athx-2027-workouts/metcon-x)).

## How to fit it into your training

Add the sandbag to 1 or 2 sessions a week, for example in a circuit: 10 sandbag-to-shoulder reps + 200 m run + 10 burpees, 4 rounds. In 6 to 8 weeks, the movement becomes natural.
