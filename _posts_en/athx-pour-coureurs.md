Half marathon, trail, 10 km: you have an engine. And ATHX appeals to you, with its run, its SkiErg and its Metcon X. Good news: a large part of the race plays to your strengths. Less good news: the **Strength zone** is unforgiving. Here's how to prepare for ATHX when you come from running.

## Your strengths

- **The Endurance zone**: a 3 km run (in ATHX and Pro), then as many SkiErg metres as possible within 24 minutes. Running fast without blowing up is what you do. See [the 2027 Endurance zone](/athx-2027-workouts/endurance-zone).
- **Managing effort**: a 2h30 race, zones to link, a pace to hold. Your experience of long races is a real advantage.
- **Metcon X**: the rower and burpees are very cardio-heavy. You'll hold the pace better than many strength athletes.

## Your weak spot: maximal strength

The [Strength zone](/athx-2027-workouts/strength-zone) counts for a third of the ranking. It tests a **1RM shoulder-to-overhead, a 2RM back squat and a 3RM deadlift**. And the ranking adds up your places in each zone: consistency matters more than a specialism. The [ATHX 2026 results](/athx-results) show it clearly: the best in a single zone rarely finish at the front.

So your goal isn't to become the strongest person in the gym, but to **not lose too many places in strength**.

## The plan for a runner

### 1. Cut back on running without stopping

Go from 4 or 5 runs to **2 or 3 a week**: one easy run, one threshold session, and one session linking running and the SkiErg. You'll keep your engine and free up time and energy for strength. The rules for combining the two well are in [combining strength training and running without losing strength](/blog/combine-strength-and-running).

### 2. Three strength sessions a week

Focused on the three scored lifts:

- [back squat](/athx-movements/back-squat);
- [deadlift](/athx-movements/deadlift);
- [shoulder-to-overhead](/athx-movements/shoulder-to-overhead).

Start **light to learn the technique**, ideally with a coach for the first few sessions. Then add a little load every week: beginners progress very quickly in the first months.

### 3. Learn the SkiErg

It's the only machine in the Endurance zone. Technique matters as much as cardio: power comes from the hips and core. See [the SkiErg: technique and pacing](/athx-movements/skierg), and estimate your metres with the [Endurance zone calculator](/athx-endurance-zone-calculator).

### 4. Choose the right category

The Metcon X loads (dumbbells, sandbag) are fixed. For a first participation, the **Lite category** is often the right choice. See [Lite, ATHX or Pro: which category to choose](/blog/choose-athx-category).

## A sample week

| Day | Session |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Strength: back squat + shoulder-to-overhead |
| Tuesday | Easy run, 40 min |
| Wednesday | Strength: deadlift + core |
| Thursday | Threshold: 4 × 1 km, or SkiErg intervals |
| Friday | Strength: shoulder-to-overhead + light squat |
| Saturday | Run then SkiErg, or a Metcon X simulation |
| Sunday | Rest |

## Common runner mistakes

- **Keeping all your running volume** and adding strength on top: fatigue builds up and strength doesn't improve.
- **Neglecting technique** to load up too fast.
- **Underestimating the sandbag** and the Metcon X loads: see [sandbag technique](/blog/sandbag-technique).
- **Never running after lifting heavy**: on race day, the run comes right after strength. I talk about it in [my Paris race report](/blog/athx-paris-2026-race-report).

## Where to start?

The simplest way: [try the free ATHX program](/free-athx-training-week). It's a full week of 5 sessions, with 4 strength sessions and a Metcon, and loads as a percentage of your PRs. If you've never measured your maxes, estimate them with the [1RM calculator](/1rm-calculator). The [ATHX PREP program](/#cta) then takes over for 12 weeks.
