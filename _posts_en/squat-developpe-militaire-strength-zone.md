The ATHX Strength zone is built around compound lifts like the **back squat** and the **strict overhead press**. Two classic exercises, but ones that cause trouble for many endurance- or functional-focused athletes.

> **2027 season update**: the Strength zone becomes a 1RM shoulder-to-overhead (leg drive allowed), a 2RM back squat and a 3RM deadlift. Details in [the ATHX 2027 Strength zone explained](/athx-2027-workouts/strength-zone). The progression principles below still apply.

## Technique matters as much as load

In competition, a badly executed rep may not count. So a load you grind up ugly in training is useless if it isn't clean. A few guidelines:

- **Back squat**: full depth (hip crease below the knee), feet flat, core braced, coming up without the torso collapsing.
- **Strict overhead press**: no leg drive, arms locked out at the top, bar over mid-foot.

Check the movement standard for your edition: that's the reference.

## Structuring your progression

Over a 12-week preparation, a simple and effective approach is to train with **percentages of your 1RM** (your one-rep max):


| Weeks | Intensity | Sets × reps |
| -------- | --------- | ----------------------------- |
| 1–3 | 65–72% | 5 × 5 |
| 4–6 | 72–80% | 5 × 3 to 5 |
| 7–9 | 80–87% | 4 × 3 |
| 10–11 | 87–92% | 3 × 2, then a few singles |
| 12 | 60–70% | deload before the competition |


The idea: raise intensity gradually, then cut volume in the last week to arrive fresh. No need to test a new record on race day.

For the technical detail, see [the back squat](/athx-movements/back-squat) and [the shoulder-to-overhead](/athx-movements/shoulder-to-overhead), the lift that replaces the strict press in 2027.

## Why start from your PRs

Two athletes who squat 80 kg and 140 kg can't follow the same program. Training with percentages ensures every session is heavy enough to progress, without burning you out. If your PRs are old, start by [testing them without getting injured](/blog/test-your-prs-safely).
