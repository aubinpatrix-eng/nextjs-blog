ATHX lasts about 2h30 from start to finish. Even though not every minute is flat out, your body is working the whole time. The Endurance zone, with running and rowing back to back, is at the heart of it. And it's trained very differently from a 10 km race.

> **2027 season update**: the Endurance zone becomes a **fixed-distance run (3 km in ATHX and Pro), followed by as many SkiErg metres as possible**, within a 24-minute cap. The principles below still apply: swap the rower for the SkiErg in your sessions and practise switching from running to the SkiErg. All the details in [the ATHX 2027 Endurance zone](/athx-2027-workouts/endurance-zone).

## Fast ≠ enduring

Many athletes train with short, intense intervals. That's useful, but not enough: what makes the difference here is the ability to **hold a steady pace** for a long time, then go again on another effort.

## The three key sessions

1. **Aerobic base** (1 to 2 times a week): 40 to 60 minutes at a pace where you can hold a conversation. It's the foundation that improves your recovery between zones.
2. **Tempo** (once a week): 20 to 30 minutes at a "comfortably hard" pace, alternating running and rowing, for example 4 × (1 km run + 1,000 m row).
3. **Combination** (every 1 to 2 weeks): a strength session followed straight away by a run/row block. It's the only way to learn to run on heavy legs.

## Common mistakes

- **Going out too fast**: the first 5 minutes should feel easy.
- **Neglecting the rower**: technique (legs, then back, then arms) saves a huge amount of energy. A bad stroke tires you out for nothing.
- **Never training tired**: if you only run fresh, you'll discover heavy legs in competition.

## In practice
