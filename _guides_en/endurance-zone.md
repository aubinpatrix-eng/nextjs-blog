## The 2027 format

The Endurance zone comes right after the Refuel zone. In 2027, it has **two parts, within a 24-minute cap**:

1. **A fixed-distance run**: **3 km** for ATHX and Pro athletes in the individual format. The distance is shorter in Lite: check it in the official workouts.
2. **As many SkiErg metres as possible** in the time left.

The score is the **total distance**. The faster you run, the more time you have left to ski, but the more tired you are when you reach the SkiErg. It's all about balance.

### In pairs

Both athletes start the run together. **As soon as the first one has covered the distance, they can start skiing.** So pairs should send their best runner to the SkiErg first.

## The right pacing strategy

### The run: fast but not flat out

Aim for a pace you could hold for **30 to 40 minutes**: fast enough to save time, without blowing up. The first 500 metres should feel easy, your legs are coming out of the Strength zone.

### The transition: every second counts

Find your SkiErg before the start, set the damper in advance if allowed, and practise switching from running to skiing in under 20 seconds.

### The SkiErg: steady to the end

Pick a pace per 500 m that you can hold, then speed up over the last few minutes. Technique matters: power comes from the hips and core, not just the arms. See [SkiErg technique](/athx-movements/skierg).

To find out how many metres you'll cover at your paces, use our [Endurance zone pace calculator](/athx-endurance-zone-calculator): it also shows what you gain by running 10 or 20 seconds per km faster.

## How to train for it

- **Two running sessions a week**: an easy aerobic run and a threshold session (for example 3 × 1 km at race pace).
- **One combination session** every one to two weeks: a 3 km run straight into 10 to 15 minutes of SkiErg.
- **Regular SkiErg work**: short intervals (8 × 250 m) and long blocks (2 × 10 min) to build power.

The general principles are covered in [building an engine that lasts 2h30](/blog/running-rowing-athx-endurance). And for the mental side of linking it with strength: [the strength → endurance transition](/blog/strength-to-endurance-transition).
