## The 1RM from A to Z: the complete guide

The 1RM is the benchmark measure of strength in the weight room. This guide explains what it is, what it's for, how to calculate it safely and how to turn the calculator's result into real training sessions.

### What is a 1RM?

The **1RM** (*one-repetition maximum*) is the heaviest load you can lift **only once** on a given movement, with correct technique and full range of motion.

On the same principle, you'll also hear about:

- **2RM**: the maximum load lifted twice in a row;
- **3RM**: the maximum load lifted three times in a row;
- **5RM**: the maximum load lifted five times in a row.

#### 1RM vs PR: what's the difference?

Your **PR** (*personal record*) is your best performance to date. Your **1RM** is your **current** maximum, tested or estimated. A PR from two years ago may no longer reflect your level: that's why you recalculate your 1RM at the start of each training cycle.

### What is the 1RM used for?

#### Programming loads as a percentage

Most strength programs express loads as a **percentage of 1RM** (for example 5 × 3 at 80%). This makes every session heavy enough to progress without wearing you out, whatever your level. Two athletes who squat 80 kg and 140 kg can follow the same plan, with loads that fit them. Full example in [back squat and overhead press: the 12-week progression](/blog/squat-overhead-press-strength-zone).

#### Tracking your progress

Recalculating your 1RM every 8 to 12 weeks lets you measure your progress objectively and readjust your training loads.

#### Choosing your attempts in competition

At ATHX, the 2027 Strength zone is a **1RM shoulder-to-overhead, a 2RM back squat and a 3RM deadlift**. Knowing these loads before race day lets you pick a safe first attempt, then build up gradually. All the details in [the ATHX 2027 Strength zone explained](/athx-2027-workouts/strength-zone).

### How do you calculate your 1RM?

#### Method 1: the direct test

You build up gradually to the maximum load you can lift once. It's the most accurate method, but also the most demanding and the riskiest. Keep it for experienced lifters, with a progressive warm-up and a spotter. Full protocol in [testing your PRs without getting injured](/blog/test-your-prs-safely).

#### Method 2: estimating with a formula

You do a heavy set of **2 to 6 reps** and estimate your 1RM with a formula. That's what the calculator does, averaging two well-known formulas:

- **Epley**: 1RM = load × (1 + reps ÷ 30)
- **Brzycki**: 1RM = load × 36 ÷ (37 − reps)

**Example**: 90 kg lifted 4 times.

- Epley: 90 × (1 + 4 ÷ 30) = **102 kg**
- Brzycki: 90 × 36 ÷ 33 = **98.2 kg**
- Average rounded to 2.5 kg: **100 kg**

#### Which method should you choose?


| Criterion | Direct test | Estimate (calculator) |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Accuracy | Maximum | Very good between 2 and 6 reps |
| Injury risk | Higher | Low |
| Fatigue | High | Moderate |
| Who for? | Experienced lifters | All levels |
| When? | Outside competition periods | Any time, even mid-preparation |


### How to use the 1RM calculator, step by step

1. **Warm up** for 10 to 15 minutes, then do 2 to 3 ramp-up sets on the chosen lift.
2. **Do a heavy set** of 2 to 6 clean reps, stopping before technical failure.
3. **Choose the unit** (kilograms or pounds), then enter the load and the number of reps in the calculator at the top of the page.
4. **Read your estimated 1RM**, along with your 2RM, 3RM and 5RM.
5. **Repeat** for the squat, the deadlift and the overhead press in the "Your 3 strength lifts" block: you get an estimate of your ATHX Strength zone total.

### Reading and using the results

#### The percentage table

Each intensity zone matches a training goal:


| % of 1RM | Reps per set | Goal |
| ---------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 90 to 100% | 1 to 2 | Maximal strength, peaking before competition |
| 80 to 90% | 2 to 5 | Strength |
| 70 to 80% | 5 to 8 | Strength and volume |
| 60 to 70% | 8 to 12 | Technique, speed, returning to training |


#### Your estimated 2RM, 3RM and 5RM

These values give you direct benchmarks for competition and for your "heavy" sessions. In practice, start your competition attempts at around **90% of the estimated value**, then adjust based on how you feel.

### The limits of an estimate

- **Beyond 6 to 8 reps**, the formulas become less accurate: muscular endurance skews the calculation.
- **A set done while tired** (after a cardio session, for example) underestimates your 1RM.
- **Technique matters**: a set with partial range of motion overestimates your real level.
- **Every lift is different**: your squat 1RM says nothing about your overhead press. Calculate them separately.
- **For beginners**, progress is fast: recalculate your 1RM more often, every 4 to 6 weeks.

### Safety best practices

- Use **safety bars** or a **spotter** on the squat and overhead lifts.
- Never go for a new max in the **10 days before a competition**.
- Stop the set as soon as technique breaks down: a grinded, ugly rep doesn't help the estimate.
- If you have pain or any doubt about your health, see a professional before training heavy.

Knowing your 1RM is only a starting point. To progress, you need to fit it into a structured plan: gradually increasing percentages, alternating with endurance work, and a deload week before the competition.
